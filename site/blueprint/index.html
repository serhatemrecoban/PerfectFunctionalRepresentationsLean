How the ideas connect

The starting question is informational: when can independent randomness represent a channel and attain the information bound? The main argument turns this into a structural question, then into finite linear constraints. The binary optimum is a separate, quantitative branch.

Shared language · Sections I–II

From auxiliary randomness to a random decoder

A decoder vector W records the output chosen for every possible input. For finite-valued X, Y and auxiliary Z, Lemma 1 preserves information when replacing Z by this vector. Perfectness is the Markov equality case of the information identity; Definition 2 expresses channel perfectness through one vector law and one common posterior for every input row.

Binary question

How informative can a representation be?

Lemma 3 combines canonicalization, mismatch entropy and maximal coupling to attain H(Y) − rH(X), where r is the total variation distance between the two channel rows. The binary case of Lemma 2 identifies when this reaches the perfect bound.

This is a special-case result, not a prerequisite for the general characterizations below. Its channel-wide equality converse requires full input support.

General question

Which channels admit a perfect representation?

Lemma 2 first forces singularity: all positive entries in a column agree. Singularity suffices for binary input or binary output, but the singular, nonperfect 3 × 3 example shows why a stronger criterion is needed in general.

The example motivates the next question; it is not a proof prerequisite. Column values and support patterns (Definition 4) supply its finite coordinates.

What the proofs depend on

Canonical witnesses and preservation give Theorem 1.
A perfect witness supplies the finite refinement that constructs a path from the seed. In the other direction, the seed is perfect and Lemma 5 transports perfectness through PPS, VPS and PPM. Witnessed inverse operations give Lemma 6 and the reduction-to-seed criterion. The construction, not preservation alone, supplies the difficult direction.
Lemma 4 explains the operation-to-partition connection.
The paper uses normal form in Section IV-A: split the seed into weighted copies (PPS), split each copy into a partition (VPS), then merge equal patterns (PPM). Lean proves this general normal-form theorem using finite certificates. Separately, its Theorem 1 constructs ordered stages directly; its LP necessity proof extracts partition weights from decoder fibres. Neither invokes the general normalizer. Conversely, feasible weights are realized by the same three stages. See why the normalization proof needed clarification.
Equality feasibility gives the primal criterion by saturation.
Row normalization bounds every feasible primal value by one. Attaining one forces all nonnegative slack to vanish, recovering Hw = v. Conversely, equality weights already attain one. This is the bridge to Theorem 2.
The dual criterion also needs a separation argument.
A primal witness of value one, weak duality and a row-derived dual witness give dual minimum one for a perfect channel. For a nonperfect channel, a separate strict-separation argument gives a feasible dual value below one (with the empty partition family handled directly). Together these prove Corollary 1.

Semantic foundations

The representation problem

A functional representation of a pair (X, Y) consists of auxiliary randomness Z, independent of X, and a decoder satisfying Y = f(X, Z) on the support of the joint law. For finite value alphabets, the information identity is

I(Y; Z) = H(Y | X) − I(X; Z | Y).

A representation is perfect when it attains the upper bound H(Y | X). Equivalently, X → Y → Z is a Markov chain. Independence and deterministic decoding remain part of the representation contract; an information equality by itself is not its definition.

Convention: mathlib PMFs and LeanInfoTheory channels; information in nats. Information statements use finite value alphabets, but the underlying probability source need not be finite.

Canonical representations

Definition 1 · A random decoder vector

A canonical representation uses a random function W from inputs to outputs, independent of X, and recovers Y by evaluation: Y = W(X) on support. A vector law whose coordinate marginals equal every channel row is a canonical coupling.

Correct coordinate marginals alone do not ensure that an already fixed triple satisfies independence and evaluation decoding. Conversely, canonicality for a fixed pair constrains channel rows only on its input support. The formalization keeps these contracts separate.

Lemma 1 · Preserve information by canonicalization

Every finite-auxiliary functional representation has a canonical replacement with the same pair law and the same mutual information with Y.

Proof idea and Lean statement

For each auxiliary value, form the entire decoder vector W(x) = f(x, Z). Independence and decoding follow directly. The representation identity expresses the change in mutual information as a difference of conditional mutual informations. Data-processing inequalities force the two sides to have opposite signs, so both vanish.

Boundary: X, Y and the original auxiliary Z are finite-valued. The replacement lives on the same probability source. The unrestricted-auxiliary reading of the paper is not fully covered.

Perfect channels and singularity

Definition 2 · One common posterior

A channel is perfectly representable when one canonical-vector law and one posterior law given the output factor the coordinate joint laws for every input row. This is a prior-free property of the channel, not a property of one chosen input distribution.

For finite alphabets, this gives perfect canonical representations for every prior. Conversely, recovering the whole channel from a single pair law requires full input support, or restriction to that prior's actual support.

Definition 3 · Singular channels

A channel is singular when all nonzero entries in any one output column have the same value. The predicate itself requires neither finiteness nor an input prior.

IsSingularChannel ↗ API

Lemma 2 · Necessary, and sufficient for binary sides

Perfect representability implies singularity. Conversely, singularity suffices for exactly two inputs or at most two active outputs. It is not sufficient in general; the formalized 3 × 3 counterexample separates the two properties.

Proof idea and Lean statements

A supported vector from the common posterior compares any two nonzero entries in one column, proving necessity. For binary input, a maximal-coupling construction provides a common posterior. For binary output, singularity reduces the channel to an equal-row or deterministic case with an explicit witness.

Boundary: necessity needs nonempty input but not finite alphabets. The binary converses use finite alphabets and nonempty input. Active outputs are the union of all row supports, not the outputs seen under an arbitrary degenerate prior.

Lemma 3 · The binary information optimum

max I(Y; Z) = H(Y) − TV(κ₀, κ₁) H(X)TV(p, q) = ½ ∑y |p(y) − q(y)|.

The maximum ranges over finite-auxiliary functional representations of a prescribed binary-input pair law. A finite representation attains it. Furthermore, TV(κ₀, κ₁) H(X) = I(X; Y) for every prior of a singular binary channel; the converse requires full input support.

Proof idea and Lean statements

With full input support, canonicalization turns a binary decoder vector into a coupling of both channel rows. The conditional output entropy H(Y | W) is the mismatch probability times H(X). Every coupling's mismatch probability is at least TV, and the explicitly constructed maximal coupling attains TV. For a degenerate binary prior, H(X) = 0 and the universal bound follows directly from I(Y; Z) ≤ H(Y). The maximal-coupling construction supplies attainment for every prior. The equality case then combines attainment with perfectness and the singularity criterion.

Finite channel geometry

Definition 4 · Values and patterns

An active column of a singular channel is its positive common value times the 0/1 indicator of its nonempty input-support pattern. Zero columns have no active-column value. Stochastic normalization remains row-wise: the sum of all distinct pattern values need not equal one.

Four legal operations

PPS
Split a column proportionally with 0 < t < 1, retaining its pattern.
PPM
Merge two columns with the same pattern.
VPS
Split a column's support into two nonempty parts, retaining its value.
VPM
Merge equal-value columns with disjoint supports.

Operation domain: finite nonempty input and active finite singular channels. Legality, singularity, exact column order and row normalization are retained by the formal operation contracts.

Lemma 4 · Ordered normal form

Every finite PPS/VPS/PPM cascade starting from an active singular channel admits three homogeneous blocks, with the same endpoint up to an explicit output bijection.

  1. PPSProportional splits
  2. VPSSupport splits
  3. PPMProportional merges
Proof idea and Lean statement

Induct on the original finite path to build a weighted and support-splitting certificate. Exact fragment coordinates record how the endpoint is assembled. Structural traversals realize the finite splitting and gathering trees in three homogeneous stages.

This proves existence of an ordered cascade, not termination of every expanding rewrite strategy, uniqueness or confluence. See the normalization discussion for why the proof was clarified.

What operations preserve

Lemma 5 · Perfectness

Legal PPS, PPM and VPS operations preserve perfect representability.

Proof idea and three Lean statements

Transport the full canonical-vector law and common posterior. PPS uses one shared Bernoulli choice for the whole vector. PPM collapses labels and mixes posteriors by the fixed column-value ratio. VPS applies the support mask to the entire vector. Each construction verifies the complete joint factorization, rather than just its marginals.

Lemma 6 · Nonperfectness

Legal PPS, PPM and VPM operations preserve nonperfect representability. Notice the change from VPS to VPM.

Proof idea and three Lean statements

If a transformed channel were perfect, its witnessed inverse operation and Lemma 5 would recover a perfect original channel, a contradiction. Recoveries remember the required ratio or support mask and an explicit output relabeling. No globally inverse function without these witnesses is asserted.

Theorem 1 · Constructive characterization

An active finite channel is perfectly representable exactly when it can be built from the one-output all-ones channel by PPS/VPS/PPM operations. Equivalently, it reduces to that seed by PPS/VPM/PPM operations.

Proof idea and both Lean characterizations

The easy directions follow from the perfect seed and preservation. For the constructive direction, a perfect canonical-vector witness supplies a common auxiliary channel. One concrete path reaches this auxiliary from the original channel; another reaches it from the seed. Compose one path with the witnessed reverse of the other.

The implementation directly packages ordered stages for this particular construction. It does not invoke the general Lemma 4 normalizer as a proof prerequisite.

From constructions to linear constraints

The partition-incidence bridge

Compress equal column patterns. Index the exact partitions of the input set whose blocks occur among those patterns. In this blueprint, H denotes the reduced pattern–partition incidence matrix and v the positive pattern-value vector. Perfectness is equivalent to nonnegative equality feasibility:

Hw = v,   w ≥ 0.

Per-input normalization forces every such solution to satisfy ∑j wj = 1. A perfect canonical witness supplies partition weights; feasible weights conversely construct a perfect channel through finite legal operations.

Equality-feasibility statement in LeanIncidence.isPerfectlyRepresentableChannel_iff_exists_feasible ↗ API

Coordinates: the full nonempty-pattern system and reduced occurring-pattern system have different indices, connected by explicit bridges. The notation H here is a local reduced-system convention; see the manuscript indexing clarification.

Theorem 2 · The primal criterion

A finite singular channel with nonempty input is perfectly representable exactly when its reduced primal has attained greatest value one:

max { ∑j wj : w ≥ 0, Hw ≤ v } = 1.
Proof idea and Lean statement

Row-wise normalization bounds every feasible objective by one. If that value is attained, all nonnegative slack coordinates vanish: the inequalities become Hw = v. Equality feasibility characterizes perfectness. Conversely, an equality witness is primal-feasible and has objective one.

Corollary 1 · The dual criterion

Under the same finite, nonempty-input and singularity assumptions, perfectness is equivalent to the reduced dual having attained least value one:

min { vᵀy : y ≥ 0, Hᵀy ≥ 1 } = 1.The right-hand 1 denotes the all-ones vector.
Proof idea and Lean statements

A primal witness of value one and weak duality bound every dual value below by one; a row-derived dual witness attains one. For nonperfect data, strict separation from the finite incidence-column convex hull gives a certificate. A cardinality-based correction makes its coordinates nonnegative, and a positive rescaling gives a feasible dual value strictly below one. Empty admissible-partition families are handled separately.
