Proof feedback · Lemma 4

A finite construction for reordering operations (Lemma 4)

Lemma 4 shows that a finite cascade of proportional splits (PPS), support splits (VPS) and proportional merges (PPM) on a singular channel can be replaced by one with all PPS operations first, then VPS, then PPM. The formalization proves this reordering lemma, but uses a different argument from repeatedly exchanging out-of-order operations.

Why counting out-of-order pairs is not enough

For a fixed list, sorting can be justified by showing that each exchange reduces the number of inversions. Here, however, a valid local interchange may replace a short segment with a longer one. Removing one out-of-order pair can introduce new pairs involving other operations. The original cascade is finite, but the list being rewritten need not keep its original length.

The missing step is therefore a justification that the proposed rewrites eventually stop. This is a gap in that termination argument—not a counterexample to Lemma 4, nor evidence that a particular rewriting strategy runs forever.

Construct the ordered cascade directly

The Lean proof instead records how finitely many weighted fragments of the original columns assemble into the final columns. This record is a factorization certificate: finite trees describe proportional splitting, restriction to input supports, and gathering fragments into outputs.

  1. Build the certificate along the original path. Each original operation extends the record while preserving the identities that reconstruct its endpoint.
  2. Realize the certificate in the desired order. Traverse its finite trees to produce a PPS-only path, then a VPS-only path, then a PPM-only path.
  3. Match the target channel. The coordinate identities and an explicit output bijection identify the constructed endpoint with the required one.

Recursion follows shorter predecessors of the original path and subtrees of finite certificates—not an expanding rewritten path. The generated certificate and cascade may grow without affecting this termination argument; no decreasing inversion count is needed.

Formalization thus supplies a constructive proof of the intended lemma while identifying an argument that the paper can strengthen. It does not establish uniqueness of the ordered cascade, termination of every rewrite strategy, or an algorithmic complexity bound.

A paper-style proof of Lemma 4

Proof. Let M be a singular conditional probability matrix with finite alphabets and at least one input symbol, and let N be obtained from M by a finite cascade of PPS, VPS and PPM operations. We work with active columns, meaning that no column is identically zero. Write the columns of M as cipi, where ci > 0 and pi is a nonzero binary vector. We construct an equivalent cascade in the required order by keeping track of how pieces of these original columns contribute to each intermediate matrix.

Each allowed operation preserves singularity and activity. Hence the columns of every intermediate matrix Q can be written as djqj, with dj > 0 and nonzero binary patterns qj. We claim that, for every original column i, there is a finite family of positive weights wia summing to one. For each weighted copy a, there is a finite family of nonzero, pairwise disjoint binary patterns piab whose sum is pi. Thus

∑awia=1, ∑bpiab=pi.

Assign each piece ciwiapiab a destination column ℓ(i, a, b) of Q, with the following two properties:

piab=qℓ(i,a,b), Q[:,j]=∑ℓ(i,a,b)=jciwiapiab.

In other words, every piece has exactly the pattern of its destination, and the pieces assigned to a column sum to that column. We retain finite binary trees recording the proportional splits that produce the weights and the support splits that produce the pattern partitions.

We establish this description by induction on the length of the original cascade. For Q = M, take one copy of weight one and one pattern pi for each column i, with destination i. Suppose now that the description holds for Q, and consider the next operation.

PPS. Suppose column l is split in the ratio α : (1 − α), where 0 < α < 1. Replace every weighted copy of every original column by two copies with weights αwia and (1 − α)wia, retaining the same pattern partition in each. For pieces with destination l, send the two copies to the two new columns, respectively. For every other piece, send both copies to its unchanged destination. Its two contributions add to the original contribution, whereas the contributions to column l are split in the required ratio. The support identities are unchanged, and all weights remain positive and sum to one. The new weight tree consists of a split with ratio α followed by a copy of the old tree on each branch.

VPS. Suppose column l is split into two nonzero, disjoint patterns ql,1 and ql,2. Every piece assigned to l has pattern ql. Replace its pattern by these same two patterns and assign the resulting pieces to the corresponding new columns, leaving its weight unchanged. Both pieces are nonzero because the given VPS is legal. All other pieces are unchanged. This refines the relevant pattern partitions, preserves the column-sum identities, and adds one binary support split at each affected leaf.

PPM. Suppose two columns with the same pattern are merged. Leave all weights and pattern partitions unchanged, and redirect the pieces assigned to either column to the merged column. Their patterns still agree with that of their destination, and their total contribution is the sum of the two original columns. Hence the required description is preserved.

Applying the induction to the whole cascade gives such a description of N. We now realize it in three stages. First, perform the recorded proportional splits on each original column, obtaining the columns ciwiapi. Second, perform the recorded support splits on each weighted copy, obtaining the pieces ciwiapiab. Every support split is legal: both branches contain nonzero parts of the corresponding partition. The first stage therefore uses only PPS operations, and the second only VPS operations.

Finally, for each column j of N, merge all pieces whose destination is j. These pieces are positive multiples of the same nonzero pattern, so they can be merged successively using PPM operations. Every target column receives at least one piece, since it is nonzero and equals the sum of its assigned pieces. The column-sum identity shows that the resulting column is precisely N[:, j]. Thus the endpoint is N up to a permutation of its output columns.

The construction is finite: the description is built by induction on the given finite cascade, and each of its recorded binary trees is finite. Traversing these trees and merging the finite destination groups produces a finite PPS–VPS–PPM cascade; any of the three blocks may be empty. This proves the lemma. ∎

Mathematical boundaries

Finite auxiliaries and support assumptions

Finite values, not a finite probability space

The basic representation definitions and decoder-vector construction do not require a finite-valued auxiliary. The information-preserving canonicalization and binary information bound do: their Lean contracts assume finite-valued X, Y and Z. The underlying space on which these random variables are defined may still be infinite.

The paper explicitly restricts X and Y to finite alphabets without explicitly imposing the same restriction on Z. Its unrestricted-auxiliary reading is therefore not fully covered. Constructing a finite representation that attains the binary bound does not by itself prove the bound for every infinite-valued auxiliary. This is a limit of the formalized coverage, not evidence that such an auxiliary can improve the optimum.

What a joint law can—and cannot—determine

Two channels can differ on an input that never occurs and still induce the same joint distribution. Consequently, a result about one pair distribution cannot automatically determine the entire channel. The formalization distinguishes pair-level results from prior-free channel results; the relevant converses either require full input support or apply to the channel restricted to the actual support.

The same care applies to decoding and conditioning. A decoder must reconstruct Y on the support of the joint law. Conditioning on a probability-zero event does not determine a distribution, although LeanInfoTheory's total conditional channel supplies a documented fallback there. The proofs preserve this convention, with null fibers contributing zero to probability-weighted identities. Outputs unused by every channel row are likewise treated explicitly when passing to active-output matrix encodings.

Representation choices

Full and reduced LP formulations

A singular channel's columns determine input-support patterns. With k inputs, there are 2k − 1 possible nonempty patterns, but only m of them may occur in a particular channel. This distinction matters when writing the incidence matrix: the full system has a row for every possible pattern and a column for every partition of the input alphabet. The reduced system keeps only occurring patterns and partitions whose every block occurs.

These are related systems, not interchangeable index sets. In the full nonnegative equality system, equations for absent patterns force the weights of inadmissible partitions to vanish. Restricting the remaining coordinates—or extending them back by zero—gives the precise bridge to the reduced system. This is an equality-feasibility equivalence, not a claim that arbitrary LPs remain equivalent after deleting rows and columns.

The formalization follows the clarified H and Ĥ indexing, consistent with the inspected LP section of the extended companion rather than a literal reading of the mixed ISIT notation. It preserves the intended representability criterion while making the coordinate choices explicit.

For the channels covered by the characterization, perfect representability is equivalent to the primal attaining a maximum of 1, and to the dual attaining a minimum of 1. Thus the dual genuinely provides an alternative mathematical criterion. Its proof uses weak duality, a value-1 witness and a specialized finite convex-separation argument—not a general LP strong-duality theorem. Numerical solving and computational performance are outside the claim.

Scope & provenance

Coverage and manuscript provenance

The target is the six-page ISIT 2026 paper On Perfect Functional Representations. The blueprint presents its definitions, lemmas, theorems, corollary and examples alongside their precise formal counterparts, including the qualifications discussed above.

The extended manuscript supplies proofs and clarification, but is not a second, fully formalized target. Extended version 16 was the proof companion for the completed development. Selected proof passages and the LP clarification were later compared with the newer companion; that comparison does not establish coverage of the entire newer draft.

The boundary is deliberate. Cited channel-simulation results from other papers, a general functional-representation existence theorem, and extended-only developments on complexity, hypergraphs and broader information measures are not formalized here.

Nor does every explanatory sentence become a public Lean theorem. Some claims occur inside proofs or as checked examples; others are omitted or require an explicit interpretation. The coverage ledger and omission register record these distinctions and interpretations.

The library case study

What LeanInfoTheory contributed

The project was built on an unchanged release of LeanInfoTheory. Mathlib supplies probability mass functions and the foundational mathematics; LeanInfoTheory supplies the channel convention, real-valued information quantities in nats, chain rules, data processing, and connections between information, independence and Markov structure.

This repository adds the paper-specific mathematics: functional representations, channel operations, finite certificates and LP characterizations. Keeping these layers separate is the central lesson of the case study. A research formalization can reuse an information-theory library without introducing a parallel probability theory or expanding the library around one paper.
