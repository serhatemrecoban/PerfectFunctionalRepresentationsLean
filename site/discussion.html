Proof feedback · Lemma 4

From local rearrangements to a finite construction.

Lemma 4 gives an ordered normal form for a finite cascade of channel operations: proportional splits (PPS), then support splits (VPS), then proportional merges (PPM). The intended theorem is formalized. Its justification, however, needs more than repeatedly fixing an out-of-order pair.

Why the informal stopping argument needs care

Some valid local interchanges replace a short segment with a longer one. Resolving a selected inversion therefore does not by itself show that the total number of inversions decreases: new operations may create new pairs elsewhere. Finiteness of the initial cascade alone does not prove termination of repeated expanding rewrites.

This identifies an incomplete termination justification, not a false normal-form theorem. It also does not demonstrate that a particular rewrite strategy is nonterminating.

What the formal proof does instead

The proof builds a finite factorization certificate while inducting on the original operation path. Proportional-split trees record positive weights; support-split trees record how weighted fragments are restricted; gathering trees assign those fragments to the final output columns. Exact coordinate identities show that the fragments reconstruct the original endpoint.

  1. Process the original finite path. Extend the certificate for each original operation, preserving its coordinate and support invariants.
  2. Realize the finite trees. Structural traversals produce three homogeneous paths: PPS, then VPS, then PPM.
  3. Identify the endpoint exactly. An explicit output bijection carries the constructed channel to the required target.

Termination comes from recursion on the original finite path and finite trees, not a claimed decreasing inversion count on rewritten paths. The result does not assert that every rewriting strategy terminates, that the normal form is unique, or that an executable normalization algorithm has a particular complexity.

Mathematical boundaries

Finite auxiliaries. Explicit support.

What “finite” restricts

The structural definitions allow unrestricted carriers. The information-preserving canonicalization and binary information bound, however, require finite-valued X, Y and auxiliary Z in their Lean contracts. The underlying probability source space need not be finite.

The paper explicitly makes X and Y finite but leaves an unrestricted reading for Z. That stronger auxiliary reading is not fully covered by this case study. A finite witness attaining an optimum does not, on its own, prove a universal information bound for every unrestricted auxiliary.

Why support cannot be hidden

A decoder must reconstruct Y where the joint law has support. Likewise, a fixed input prior cannot reveal what a channel does on input rows assigned probability zero. The formalization therefore separates statements about a pair distribution, all-prior channel statements, and converses requiring full input support.

Null conditional fibers retain LeanInfoTheory's total-channel convention. Zero-probability outputs and explicit active-output encodings are handled at the semantic-to-matrix boundary rather than discarded silently.

See the complete boundary index for finite/nonempty alphabets, null fibers, coordinate orientation and other contract details.

Representation choices

Precise coordinates, the same intended criterion.

The LP statements distinguish the full system of nonempty input-support patterns from a reduced system of patterns that actually occur. With k inputs, the former has 2k − 1 pattern coordinates; the effective reduced system may have only m occurring patterns. These are related formulations, not interchangeable index sets.

The formalization makes the full/reduced incidence distinction and its bridges explicit. It follows the clarified H and Ĥ indexing, consistent with the inspected LP section of the extended companion, rather than a literal reading of the mixed ISIT notation.

The primal and dual criteria include attainment. Their intended use as alternative mathematical characterizations is formalized. General-purpose LP infrastructure, numerical solving, and computational performance claims are not part of this result.

Scope & provenance

A bounded case study, not the entire extended draft.

The primary target is the exact six-page ISIT 2026 manuscript On Perfect Functional Representations. Extended version 16 supplied the historical proof and clarification baseline for the sixteen completed chunks. Selected proof passages and the LP clarification in the newer companion were subsequently compared; the entire newer draft was not revalidated.

The project covers the agreed definitions, lemmas, theorems, corollary, nonrepresentable example and supporting constructions. It intentionally does not re-formalize cited channel-simulation results from other papers, a general functional-representation existence theorem, or extended-only complexity, hypergraph and broader information-measure developments.

Not every informal sentence corresponds to a public Lean declaration. Some are proof-internal facts or checked fixtures; others have a documented omission or interpretation. The coverage ledger and omission register retain those distinctions.

The library case study

Reuse the foundations. Keep the paper downstream.

Mathlib provides PMFs and the foundational mathematical infrastructure. LeanInfoTheory provides the channel convention, Real-valued information quantities in nats, chain rules, data processing and bridges between zero information, independence and Markov structure.

PFR builds paper-specific representations, matrix operations, finite certificates and LP characterizations on that foundation. It does not introduce a parallel probability theory or require the library to grow around this one paper.
