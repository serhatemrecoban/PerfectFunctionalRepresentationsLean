01 / SEMANTICS

Representations & canonicalization

The information identity

A representation's information shortfall is exactly a conditional mutual information:

I(Y; Z) = H(Y | X) − I(X; Z | Y)

It is perfect precisely when X → Y → Z forms a Markov chain.

Contract: finite value alphabets X, Y, Z; an arbitrary PMF source; support-only deterministic decoding. All information quantities are in nats.

Information-preserving canonicalization · Lemma 1

Replace the auxiliary by its vector of decoder outputs, one for each input. This canonical representation preserves the pair law and the mutual information with Y.

Contract: finite X, Y and auxiliary Z; the source space itself need not be finite. This does not cover the manuscript's unrestricted-auxiliary reading.

02 / SPECIAL CASES

Singularity & binary optima

Singularity: necessary, sometimes sufficient · Lemma 2

Every perfectly representable channel is singular. Conversely, singularity suffices when there are exactly two inputs or at most two active outputs. It is not sufficient for general alphabets: the project also verifies the paper's nonrepresentable 3 × 3 example.

Contract: necessity requires nonempty input, without an input prior or finiteness hypothesis. The displayed binary-side converse uses finite alphabets and nonempty input.

Singularity declarations
IsPerfectlyRepresentableChannel.isSingular ↗isPerfectlyRepresentableChannel_iff_isSingular_of_binary ↗

An exact binary information optimum · Lemma 3

max I(Y; Z) = H(Y) − TV(κ₀, κ₁) H(X)

The maximum ranges over finite-auxiliary functional representations of the prescribed pair law. Here TV is the half-L1 distance, ½ Σy |κ₀(y) − κ₁(y)|. The universal bound is attained by a finite representation. The construction uses a maximal coupling whose mismatch probability is exactly the total variation between the two channel rows.

Contract: binary input, finite output and auxiliary; any input prior. Attainment is constructed on a triple-law extension with the prescribed pair law, not necessarily on the original source space.

03 / OPERATIONS

An ordered normal form

Normalize a finite cascade · Lemma 4

An allowed cascade can be reorganized into three homogeneous blocks, preserving its endpoint up to an explicit output relabeling.

  1. PPSProportional splits
  2. VPSSupport splits
  3. PPMProportional merges

Contract: finite nonempty input, active finite channels, a singular starting channel, and an allowed PPS/VPS/PPM path. “Up to relabeling” is an explicit bijection, not an informal identification.

04 / STRUCTURE

A constructive characterization

Build a perfect channel from a seed · Theorem 1

A channel is perfectly representable exactly when it is obtainable from the one-output all-ones channel by the prescribed finite operations. The reverse route reduces the channel back to that seed.

Contract: finite nonempty input and active finite channel. The from-seed route uses PPS/VPS/PPM; the to-seed route uses PPS/VPM/PPM. No input-prior assumption is hidden in this channel statement.

05 / OPTIMIZATION

Primal & dual characterizations

Two attained criteria · Theorem 2 & Corollary 1

A singular channel is perfectly representable precisely when its reduced primal LP has greatest attainable value 1. Equivalently, its reduced dual LP has least attainable value 1.

Contract: finite alphabets, nonempty input and singularity. Full and reduced pattern-coordinate systems are connected by explicit mathematical bridges.

Primal and dual declarations
