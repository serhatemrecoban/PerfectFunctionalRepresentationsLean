## 3. Mathematical and representation contracts

Use namespace `PerfectFunctionalRepresentations`. Let `beta : Type u`,
`[Fintype beta]`, and `p q : PMF beta`. Use arbitrary universes and derive
nonemptiness from a supplied PMF. No public `Inhabited`, selected default atom,

### 3.1 TV, overlap and conversion

Proposed definitions, with the following precise meaning:

```text
finiteTV p q : Real := (1 / 2 : Real) * sum_y |(p y).toReal - (q y).toReal|
overlapMass p q : ENNReal := sum_y min (p y) (q y)
```

Here and below `sum_y` denotes the complete finite sum. Write `c_y` for
`min (p y) (q y)`, `a = overlapMass p q`, `d = 1 - a`,
`u_y = p y - c_y`, and `v_y = q y - c_y`, all in `ENNReal`.
These letters are mathematical notation, not promised public definitions.
Required identities and boundaries:

1. `a <= 1`, `d <= 1`, all relevant atoms/sums are finite, and
   `a + d = 1`. Exact residual sums are `sum_y u_y = d = sum_y v_y`.
2. `finiteTV p q = d.toReal = 1 - sum_y min ((p y).toReal) ((q y).toReal)`.
   Also `finiteTV p q = sum_y (p y - q y).toReal`, and the reversed
   positive-part formula. State precisely where subtraction is truncated
   (`ENNReal`) and where it is ordinary (`Real`). Do not subtract PMFs.
3. Symmetry, `0 <= finiteTV p q <= 1`, and
   `finiteTV p q = 0 <-> p = q`.
4. `finiteTV p q = 1 <-> a = 0 <-> Disjoint p.support q.support`.
   The equivalent pointwise-zero-minimum condition must be available by a
   lemma or direct consumer. Zero-common-mass is not an exceptional failure.

`toReal` injectivity/order uses explicit non-top hypotheses; finite masses
must not be inferred from a syntactically finite expression containing an
unchecked inverse. Reuse the pinned conversion lemmas before adding wrappers.

### 3.2 Couplings and mismatch

For `g : PMF (beta × beta)`, propose

```text
IsCoupling p q g := fstMarginal g = p AND sndMarginal g = q
mismatchMass g : ENNReal := sum_t (if t.1 != t.2 then g t else 0)
```

In the contracts below, `mismatchProbability g` is **mathematical shorthand**
for `(mismatchMass g).toReal`, not a proposed additional public definition.
The pinned `LeanInfoTheory.Shannon.decodingErrorProbability g id` already
represents this Real probability. A separately importable bridge must prove
their equality, then reuse the existing Real error-probability API. Core Real
theorems use the `toReal` expression, avoiding a second decoder-error theory.

`fstMarginal` and `sndMarginal` here mean the existing
`LeanInfoTheory.Shannon` operations, not new projections. Retain the product
law rather than introducing a parallel probability structure or bundling
proofs in a new coupling carrier. This two-marginal predicate is distinct
from C02's function-vector `IsCanonicalCoupling`.

Required content:

- Exact diagonal decomposition
  `mismatchMass g + sum_y g (y,y) = 1`, its complement form, and its `Real`
  finite-sum forms. Establish that the finite sum equals
  `g.toOuterMeasure {t | t.1 != t.2}`, using the existing finite PMF event API.
  This is a boundary lemma, not a second event-probability library.
- `mismatchMass g <= 1` and real range `[0,1]`.
- For **every** `g` satisfying `IsCoupling p q g`,
  `g (y,y) <= min (p y) (q y)` pointwise, diagonal mass at most `a`,
  `d <= mismatchMass g`, and
  `finiteTV p q <= mismatchProbability g`.

These results assume neither independence of the two coordinates nor a
particular decoder, input prior, source space or nonempty-support enumeration.
The independent product is an available coupling, but need not be maximal.

### 3.3 Specified maximal coupling

Construct `maximalCoupling p q : PMF (beta × beta)` with both marginals and
the following **exact all-atom contract**:

```text
(maximalCoupling p q) (y,z)
  = (if y = z then c_y else 0) + u_y * v_z / d
```

The product in the numerator is divided by **one** residual mass `d`, not
`d^2`. Its total residual mass is `d`. At `d = 0`, both residual functions
vanish, the residual term is zero under the explicit ENNReal zero arithmetic,
and the result is `p.map (fun y => (y,y))` with `p = q`. A branch using that
diagonal law is an acceptable implementation provided the all-atom theorem
still covers it. At `a = 0`, `d = 1` and the result is the existing independent
product of `p` and `q`. No normalized zero-mass PMF or default choice is used.

Required public guarantees: correct marginals; exact all-atom and diagonal
formulas; diagonal mass `a`; mismatch mass `d`; and
`mismatchProbability (maximalCoupling p q) = finiteTV p q`. Combine the
universal bound and witness into the finite minimum/attainment statement.
An existential theorem may be a short corollary; an `sInf`, `IsLeast` or
optimizer wrapper is unnecessary without a consumer.

Retain the explicit construction and support obligations, not just its
existence. Required C05-facing consequences are:

- For `y != z`, positive mass requires, and is equivalent to,
  `q y < p y` and `p z < q z`; these inequalities imply `d > 0`.
- If `p y = q y`, the two fibers with coordinate `y` have no off-diagonal
  mass, including when both masses are zero. If their common mass is positive,
  the diagonal atom has exactly that mass.
- Zero entries outside the marginal supports never acquire mass.

These can be a minimal support theorem plus anonymous consumers of the
atom formula. Do not promise a named helper for every specialization. The
conditions use masses alone; do not import or assume `IsSingularChannel`.

### 3.4 Relabeling and downstream boundary

For finite `gamma` and injective `f : beta -> gamma`, set
`G = g.map (fun t => (f t.1, f t.2))`. Prove:

- `IsCoupling p q g` implies `IsCoupling (p.map f) (q.map f) G`;
- overlap, TV, exact mismatch and real mismatch are unchanged by simultaneous
  injective relabeling;
- consequently, a maximal coupling pushes forward to an attaining coupling.

Equivalences are direct specializations. These guarantees include adding
unused output labels and transporting from one finite subtype carrying both
laws through the same inclusion, without full-support or surjectivity
assumptions. For ambient laws this common carrier can be the union of their
supports; neither support is assumed to contain the other. R12 may instead
start with two laws already on the same finite subtype, requiring no new
restriction framework. No invariance under arbitrary many-to-one maps is
asserted. Swapping the two coordinates
gives the reversed coupling and preserves mismatch; combine with TV symmetry
to remove a dependence on the order of the two laws.

Equality of the two *chosen* constructions after relabeling is not required:
C05 needs a transported attaining witness, not canonical uniqueness. C05 owns
the equivalence between `beta × beta` and a two-coordinate function vector,
its intrinsic channel specialization, entropy and input-support hypotheses.
